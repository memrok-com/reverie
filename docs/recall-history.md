# Episode recall history

## Contract selected before implementation

Fresh lexical retrieval still runs for every reply. A separate historical section preserves selected recollections actually supplied to successful replies on the current Pi branch. Supplied context is not proof of a conscious thought, an expressed reaction, or a new occurrence of the remembered event. Played dialogue alone supports claims about what happened in this episode. No per-turn lasting memories or automatic reinforcement are added.

The default historical budget is 6,000 UTF-16 code units of serialized context, including provenance, and at most eight distinct recollections. Select the four most recent distinct activations, then up to four remaining recollections by original salience, breaking ties by recency. Emit selected items in played order. Keep the first and latest turn association for each retained recollection, so repeated retrieval cannot erase the initial emotional connection. Repeated supply adds neither a frequency bonus nor salience, confidence or retrieval strength. Selection never recursively expands saved historical sections. Current retrieval has its own existing budget.

Each retained item identifies its successful reply, preceding played prompt, fictional activation time, original episode and contribution state, recollection index and original supporting message IDs. The host takes reply IDs only from the selected branch and reads their character/episode-scoped saved recall records. A played-prefix fingerprint excludes associations whose supporting dialogue or foundation has since been edited. Failed/aborted replies and rejected alternatives do not establish history. Restart/resume reuses those records. Inspection remains a player-only operation.

The native engine independently resolves references against eligible, currently published contributions. It excludes missing or changed contribution states, withdrawn/deleted memory, the current episode's own previous contribution, later episodes and activations after the current fictional point. Old explanations remain inspectable but cannot restore invalidated knowledge. Legacy explanations lacking contribution references are inspectable only. Re-memorization or rebuild can preserve references only if the published contribution identity remains identical; correction or changed regeneration invalidates them.

Deletion clears the deleted episode's records. When it has a published contribution, cleanup also covers same-character episodes at or after the earlier of its current/published starts, considering both chronologies. Independently of current publication, cleanup removes individual reply records whose fresh memories, historical context, impression sources or shared-history sources name the deleted episode. This covers withdrawn or previously invalidated memory and replies in episodes re-dated earlier. Unrelated replies in those episodes and other characters' records are preserved. A genuinely never-memorized draft has no such dependents, so its deletion clears only its own records.

A durable cleanup plan saves both affected episode IDs and referencing reply IDs before native deletion and is consumed atomically with catalog acknowledgment. Old episode-only plans remain readable. Acknowledgment checks for additional references saved after planning, and saving a late reply rejects context that refers to a known pending/completed source deletion. Restart can finish cleanup even when native source dates are already gone. Unaffected earlier histories retain their reflection inputs and activations.

Episode-end reflection receives selected played source and bounded per-turn recall associations under a separate `recallHistory` field. It must connect recalled background only to reactions supported by current played messages. Prior memory retains its original provenance; supplying it repeatedly is not additional evidence. Reflection validates history against staged earlier contributions, so withdrawn, corrected or regenerated background cannot return through historical snapshots. History participates in input validation independently of dialogue and is rechecked before publication.

Reflection history uses short episode/recollection and current-message aliases. Internal contribution hashes, memory indices, detail-supply flags and raw background source-message IDs remain available for engine validation and saved player explanations, but are omitted from reflection input. Recollection identity comes from indexed stored row order, resolved against canonical contributions within one read snapshot, rather than exact prose/float equality.

## Verification

Acceptance covers shifted retrieval after an emotional activation, branch alternatives, failures, restart/resume, fictional rewind, ownership and obsolete references after correction, withdrawal, deletion and rebuild. Focused deterministic comparisons check that history supplies the missing association without copying the old event into the new episode. These comparisons verify the context and attribution contract; they do not establish subjective quality across live models or sustained play.
